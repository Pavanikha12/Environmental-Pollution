Environmental pollution and environmental protection in the Supreme Court of India, 2021 to 2026

Team Green Justice: Pavanikha (Division B), Riddhi (Division A), Shreya (Division A)

Judgment Content Analysis project

This page analyses 26 Supreme Court of India judgments and substantive orders on pollution and environmental protection, decided between 29-11-2021 and 07-08-2026 (our search window ran from 01-09-2021 to 30-10-2026). We ask one question: has the Court's approach to pollution cases changed over these years? Every figure comes from our dataset.

What changed, and what did not

  • The core principles stay. Polluter pays (6 judgments) and precaution with sustainable development (6) are the most common principles, and each is applied in several different years. The Court's basic vocabulary did not change.
  • Results became less clear-cut. Before 2024 only one of seven records has a mixed or unclear winner. From 2024 it is ten of 19, including all three records from 2026. The Court increasingly supervises and directs regulators instead of declaring a winner.
  • More writ and continuing proceedings. All 15 civil appeals are from 2025 or earlier, and all three 2026 records are writ petitions. Nine of the ten writ petitions are from 2024 onwards.
  • Rights language is recent. The principle group "clean environment as a right; public trust" appears only from 2024 (1, 1 and 2 judgments in 2024, 2025 and 2026). Prior clearance and EIA appears in 2021, 2022 and 2025, and not in 2024 or 2026.
  • Themes cluster by year. All three waste-management records are from 2022, and all four river and water pollution records are from 2025 and 2026. Environmental clearances (7) are the largest theme and run from 2021 to 2025.
  • NGOs appeared most in 2025. Five of the nine records from 2025 were brought by associations or NGOs, against two in all other years combined. The sample is small, so this may reflect which cases we found.

The charts

1. Judgments per year

The 26 records are uneven: 2025 has nine and 2024 seven, while 2023 has only one. Fifteen are civil appeals, ten are writ petitions (including suo motu proceedings) and one is a special leave petition. These counts describe our sample, not the Court’s total environmental caseload.

2. What the Court was asked, by year

No theme runs through every year. Waste management is concentrated in 2022 and river and water pollution in 2025 and 2026, while environmental clearances are the most persistent theme (2021 to 2025).

3. Which principles were applied, by year

This is the closest we have to doctrinal evolution. Polluter pays and precaution recur, prior clearance and EIA fades after 2025, and the right-to-clean-environment and public-trust group appears only from 2024.

4. Who won, by year

There is no steady swing towards companies, regulators or petitioners. What does change is the growth of the mixed or unclear group from 2024, which is all of 2026.

5. Precedents the Court relied on

Vellore Citizens’ Welfare Forum v. Union of India is named in seven judgments, followed by Alembic Pharmaceuticals v. Rohit Prajapati (5) and Indian Council for Enviro-Legal Action v. Union of India (4). The Court keeps returning to the 1990s foundations of Indian environmental law.

6. Who brought the case and who won

Companies and NGOs each brought seven cases. Companies won five; NGOs won three. Because eleven judgments have a mixed or unclear winner, the chart understates how often each side obtained something.

7. Legislation, by year

The Water and Air Acts (11 records) are the most common basis, with sector-specific rules and the Environment (Protection) Act with the EIA framework at seven each. Pollution litigation rarely rests on one statute.

How we built this

We used the Supreme Court of India judgments portal and legal repositories including Indian Kanoon, Supreme Court Observer, VoxJuris and Law Observer. Search terms included "industrial pollution", "river pollution", "air pollution", "effluent discharge", "environmental clearance", "post-facto clearance", "fly ash", "solid waste", "wetlands", "sand mining", "pollution control board" and "environmental compensation". We did not keep a reliable count of raw results. We kept 26 judgments or substantive orders dated 01-09-2021 to 30-10-2026 that concerned pollution, its regulation or a closely connected safeguard, and that had an identifiable case name, date, citation or case number and a reported issue and result. Our grouping of themes, principles and legislation is our own reading of the dataset labels.

Limits

Twenty-six records chosen by search are not a complete list, and some are orders within continuing cases. Do not conclude from them how often the Court rules for polluters, regulators or affected communities, or that its doctrine changed. Counts of one to nine records a year can move by chance.
